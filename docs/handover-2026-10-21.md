# Algobooth handover, 2026-10-21

For a fresh session picking up where the last ones left off. Everything here is on `main` and pushed
(GitHub `TomKlimovski/algobooth`). Read this, then `README.md` and `service/README.md`.

## What it is

A native, offline DJ booth around Strudel for Tom's goal of playing a club set live-coding. Two Strudel
decks with a crossfader and master section, a library of patterns as JSON files on disk, and a Rip tab
that turns a YouTube clip into Strudel code that *emulates* it (Tom then tweaks). Tauri 2 shell (Rust),
Solid + Vite front end, a Python service (`service/`, package `algorip`) for the rip pipeline.

Run it: `pnpm tauri dev` from the repo root (the app starts and stops the Python service itself). A code
change on the service side needs a full relaunch, not Cmd+R, since the service is a separate process.

## Tom's principles (keep these)

- **Emulate, then tweak.** The rip's job is a short, readable approximation Tom can play with, never a
  wall of numbers.
- **As modular as possible.** One named line per part (`drums:`, `hats:`, `bass:`, `chords:`,
  `melody:`), the simplest loop on each line, and anything that varies as its own *muted* line
  (`_bass2:` for a build, `_fills:` for drum fills) with a comment naming the bars. Never fold a
  variation into a loop to be "more faithful". A leading underscore mutes a Strudel named pattern.
- **Never `.orbit()` in generated code.** Orbits are the app's deck channels (1 = deck A, 2 = deck B,
  3 = the rip result).
- No internet needed after the one-time model download except to fetch new clips; nothing leaves the
  machine unless a Claude API key is set and Tidy is ticked.

## State of the work

Milestones 1 to 5 of the original design are built (booth, mixer, master, sync, library, settings, rip
pipeline). Since then, three rounds on the rip's output, all merged:

1. **Named lines** instead of `$:` (2026-10-20).
2. **Bass loop** (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-20-bass-loop-design.md`, plan beside it): Basic Pitch
   is no longer used for bass. `bassgrid.py` tracks pitch and onsets per 16th step with pyin;
   `loop.py` finds the period the bars repeat at with a two-pass majority vote (core bars vs deviating
   bars); `bass.py` runs a small candidate search (16th vs 8th grid, retriggered vs sustained) and
   picks the shortest notation within 0.05 of the best match; `score.py` scores against the grid with a
   frame pitch agreement and mir_eval onset F1. The build material of deviating bars becomes a muted
   `_bass2:` line, measured on sounding steps. `Analysis.bass_loop` carries it; the Rip tab shows
   `bass loop 1 bar · match 0.80`. Second pass on 2026-10-22
   (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-22-sparse-loop-design.md`): core membership is containment, the
   cleanest bars vote the template, a longer period needs a 0.2 gain, and the variation is drawn from
   every bar (one short loop when the adding bars share it, else bar by bar); the 48:50 clip is the
   second golden, `solomun2`. Third pass the same day (`2026-10-22-bass-deep-dive-design.md`), from
   measuring the 48:50 stem against the rip: a spectral second opinion in `bassgrid.py` for a loud
   step pyin reads an octave down (its beat 4 was dropped), and a measured gate envelope
   (`bass_envelope`: attack, hold, fall; written as `.attack().clip().release()` when the notes die
   before their end; the stem holds then cuts, it does not decay).
   The deep-dive scripts' approach (per-16th levels, per-note decay, harmonic spectra, cross-correlation
   for echoes, the drum stem for bleed) is the way to check every part after its loop lands.
3. **Drum loop** (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-21-drum-loop-design.md`): `drumloop.py` runs the
   same loop finder per drum voice; every hit the loop does not play, from every bar, goes into one
   muted `_fills:` line. `Analysis.drum_summary` feeds `drums loop 1 bar · 86% of hits` on the tab.
   Melody still uses the older strict `mini.consensus`; unifying it is open.
4. **Drum deep dive** (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-22-drum-deep-dive-design.md`, 2026-10-22), the
   measuring method applied to the 48:50 drums and hats. The kick is the beat: `grid.py` moves the
   tracker's beats onto the kick onsets when they agree and sit over half a step off (the 48:50 clip's
   kicks all landed 2.3 steps after its beats, so it got `[~ bd]*4` on the 808); a moved grid's downbeat
   goes by the backbeat (claps on 2 and 4) when the kicks are equal, an unmoved grid keeps the tracker's
   rule, so 36:40 is untouched. `kits.json` (built by `scripts/measure_kits.py` from the app's sample
   packs) holds every 909/808 sample's level and the time of its first 12 dB of fall; `drums.drum_tone`
   measures the stem the same way and `sounds.choose_sounds` picks each voice's sample by shape
   (`bd:3`, `oh:2`, `cp:4`, written inline) and its line's gain from the stem's level against the
   kick's. Lines are one per voice so each carries its gain: `drums:` (kick), `claps:`/`snare:`,
   `hats:`, `ghosts:` (hats 12 dB or more under the others, the 48:50 clip's 16th-note ticks), `_fills:`.
   An onset look-back for the bass (it leads the kick by 12 ms here) was built and withdrawn at review:
   it moved the readings by a frame and drifted 36:40's; `bass.py` is unchanged.

**Key latency, measured on 2026-10-23.** Tom felt "a second or two" between a key and the music. A probe in
the native app (WebKit, his settings: quantise `now`, scope visualiser, the Solomun rip loaded) put keydown to
the new pattern being live at 13 to 23 ms, the same as Chromium and Playwright's WebKit; nothing in the
key path or the evaluation is slow. What remained was Strudel's clock: sounds were scheduled 200 to 330 ms
ahead (a 100 ms tick interval plus 100 ms overlap of lookahead, then a fixed 100 ms trigger latency), so a
pattern swap was heard that much late, and then only at the changed line's next hit (a clap on 2 and 4 is up
to 930 ms away at 129 bpm, a chord per bar up to 1.86 s). The key quantise default is `bar` (1.86 s at 129),
which is the "second or two" if it was on. The clock lead is now 50 to 250 ms (`DECK_CLOCK`, gotcha below);
the wait for the next hit is the pattern's and stays. A reusable Playwright measurement (WebKit or Chromium
against the dev server: press-to-evaluation lag, scheduling lead, dropped sounds) lived in the session
scratchpad; it stubs `__TAURI_INTERNALS__` so the quantise button works in a browser tab.

## Gotchas

- **A cue is recognised from its marker.** The deck knows what is cued by the `// cued: ` comment in its code
  (that is also how a saved half-mix comes back as a mix), so a hand edit of that comment changes what the deck
  thinks is cued.

- **vitest 5 module cache.** `node_modules/.vitest-cache` keeps transformed test modules per file; after
  goldens are added or changed, run `pnpm exec vitest run --clearCache` or the golden glob test fails
  (its message says so).
- **Strudel bumps.** Re-run `pnpm build-methods` when Strudel is bumped, or `app/src/methods/catalogue.test.ts`
  fails (its message says so).
- **Strudel core is patched.** `patches/@strudel__core@1.2.6.patch` (pnpm `patchedDependencies`) lets the repl
  pass `latency`, `interval`, `clockInterval` and `clockOverlap` through to Cyclist, in `dist/index.mjs` (what the
  app loads) and the source files alike. The decks pass `DECK_CLOCK` (`app/src/engine/sync.ts`): a 50 ms tick,
  100 ms query overlap, 50 ms trigger latency, against Strudel's 100/100/100. A Strudel bump must re-apply the
  patch (`pnpm patch @strudel/core@<new>`, same four options) or the decks silently fall back to the defaults.
- **WebKit drag and drop.** `dragDropEnabled` is `false` on the window in `tauri.conf.json` and must stay so:
  with Tauri's native drag-drop handler on, wry answers every drag itself and WebKit never fires the page's
  `dragenter`/`dragover`/`drop` (the panel's methods could not be dropped on a key, and a drop over the editor
  became a plain text insertion). Chromium hand checks cannot see this; only the native app can. A drop target
  must also cancel `dragenter`, not just `dragover`, or WebKit leaves the drop with the editor it crossed.
- **WebKit.** `fetch` and `setTimeout` must be called unbound (see `ripApi.ts`); vertical range inputs
  use `writing-mode: vertical-lr`; engine singletons force a full reload on HMR
  (`import.meta.hot.invalidate()`); the AudioContext is closed on `pagehide`.
- **Repl globals.** Concurrent Strudel evaluations mix `setcpm`/`hush` globals, so every evaluation goes
  through the eval lock (`app/src/engine/evalLock.ts`). The rip result editor takes edits only from
  document changes, never from the repl's state echoes (`ResultEditor.tsx` explains why).
- **Service token.** The Rust supervisor passes a random bearer token in the environment; `GET /health`
  is the only unauthenticated route. The port comes from the service's first stdout line.
- **Commits.** Trailer `Co-Authored-By: Claude Fable 5.1 <noreply@anthropic.com>`, nothing else.
  Subagents must be told explicitly or they write an Opus trailer. Plans are executed with the
  superpowers subagent-driven-development skill in a worktree session; those sessions tend to end their
  turn while waiting on an implementer and need a nudge ("do not end your turn to wait").
- **Process.** Design docs in `docs/superpowers/specs/`, plans in `docs/superpowers/plans/`; a fresh
  builder subagent reads each design before Tom sees it; Tom approves the design, then the plan, then
  the branch is merged into `main` by fast-forward after both suites pass locally.
